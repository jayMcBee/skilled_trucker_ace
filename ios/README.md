# iPad build — SpriteKit, UIKit

Swift port of the web game, with the graphics, effects, sound and touch controls the web
build does not have. The web build in the repo root stays the physics reference.

**None of this Swift has been compiled.** It was written on Linux, where there is no Swift
toolchain and no SpriteKit. Expect to fix build errors on the first run. The physics file and
its tests are the part most likely to be right; the scene is the part most likely to need a
fix.

## Build

Open `ios/CrazySkilledTrucker.xcodeproj`, pick an iPad, `Cmd-R`. `Cmd-U` runs the tests.

The project is the Xcode 26 template with its folder synchronised to disk, so every file
under `CrazySkilledTrucker/` is in the app target and every file under
`CrazySkilledTruckerTests/` is in the test target. Adding a file means adding it to the
folder. The project is set to iPad only, landscape only, full screen, deployment target
iOS 26.5, Swift 5 mode with main-actor default isolation.

To regenerate the sounds: `python3 ios/tools/make_sounds.py`. Stdlib only.

To regenerate the trucks: `python3 ios/tools/make_trucks.py` from a venv with Pillow. It writes
`tools/cache/truck_preview.png`, every sprite at full size plus a row at the size the iPad
shows them and a row at angles.

To regenerate the floor: `python3 ios/tools/make_floor.py` from a venv with numpy and Pillow.
It fetches the CC0 asphalt tile into `tools/cache/` on the first run and writes
`tools/cache/floor_preview.png`, the whole layer stack over a mock lot, so the look can be
judged before it reaches the iPad.

## Controls

Two pads, bottom corners. Drive on the left: push up for forward, down for reverse. The
deflection is the throttle, so a small push creeps. Let go and the truck brakes. Steer on the
right: the thumb's position across the pad is the wheel angle. Let go and the wheel holds.
A thumb that lands anywhere in the lower half of the screen takes the pad on that side.

The **Options** sheet has:

- Handling preset: Standard, Forgiving, Very forgiving. Same three as the web build.
- Forward speed dial, 0.3x to 2.5x. Reverse speed dial, 0.3x to 1.6x. The km/h and the fold
  clock are printed under each dial and update as you drag.
- Jackknife ends the run. Off by default, the same as `JACKKNIFE_ENDS_RUN` in `core.js`.
- Lot edge: open, so the asphalt goes on and nothing stops the truck, or kerb, where a
  worn yellow line marks the four edges and a scrape on them ends the run. Open by default.
- Swap the pads.
- Particle effects.
- Sound engine: Off, Synth, Samples.

Changing a preset or a dial restarts the run. Everything is kept across launches.

## Graphics

The floor is a photographed asphalt tile (ambientCG Asphalt004, CC0) darkened for night, laid
as an `SKTileMapNode` in four rotations over three lots in every direction, so no edge of it
is ever on screen. A soft grime layer is multiplied over it to break the repeat. Cracks, oil
stains, tyre marks and puddles are baked decal PNGs placed by seed: oil where the tractors
stand, tyre marks at the angle of a truck swinging into a slot. Stall lines and the bay are
one strip of eroded paint, stretched and tinted. Three sodium lamps: a wide pool, a brighter
core and the lamp head. A vignette darkens the corners of the screen into night.

The screen fits the lot's own bounds, not the old 850 x 650 web canvas, into the space left
over by the pads and the HUD. By default the lot is turned across the screen: the lane runs
left to right, the truck starts at the left end facing right, and the bay is in the top row.
That is an option, because it is a matter of feel.

The walls are gone as pictures. With the open edge nothing stops the truck. With the kerb the
collision edge is a worn yellow line.

Trucks are classic 2D: one flat baked sprite per trailer and per cab, dark outline, flat
colour, the sprite just rotates. The effort went into variety. Eight liveries with invented
fleet names, each with two trailers of different types out of box, reefer with a nose unit,
curtainsider, flatbed with a tarp or a stack of pipes, tanker, and container on a chassis.
Parked trucks take their livery in fleets of one to three neighbours, the way a haulier's
trucks park together. The player drives a white box with a red cab. Every sprite is exactly
its collision box. The tail lamps keep an additive glow on top. The player's cab is baked
without front wheels; the game draws them in the baked tyres' size, place and colours and
turns them 1.3x the wheel angle, a detail that moves, not a gauge. The wedge at the nose is
the readout. Shadows fall the same way on every truck.

Effects: exhaust from the cab stack that thickens with throttle, brake lamps, white reverse
lamps, a dust puff and a clunk when the fold jams, and on a scrape: sparks, debris, dust, a
screen shake and a red flash at the contact point. Parking throws gold and green.


## Sound

Two engines, chosen in Options. Both use `AVAudioEngine` and the `.ambient` session, so the
mute switch works and music keeps playing.

**Synth**: one `AVAudioSourceNode`. The render block sums four harmonics of a fundamental that
rises with speed and throttle, adds a smoothed noise rattle, and rounds the sum with tanh.
A gated 1 kHz tone is the reverse beeper. The crash is a noise burst over a 55 Hz thump, the
jam a short 85 Hz thud, the win a two-tone horn. No files. The control values cross to the
audio thread through `Atomic` from the Synchronization module, and the one-shots are
atomic counters the render block watches.

**Samples**: `AVAudioPlayerNode` loops through `AVAudioUnitVarispeed`, so the engine loop's
pitch and tempo rise together with speed. An ambient bed, a beeper loop that plays while
reversing, and one player for the three one-shots. The WAVs are 22 kHz mono, made by
`tools/make_sounds.py`: the engine loop is a harmonic stack at 40 Hz so it loops without a
seam, the ambient bed is crossfaded brown noise.

Haptics do not apply: iPads have no Taptic Engine.

Free samples, if real recordings are wanted later: freesound.org with the CC0 filter (qubodup's
truck idle loops), kenney.nl Impact Sounds (CC0).

## Two traps

**The y-axis is flipped.** `Rig.swift` works in canvas coordinates, where +y points DOWN.
SpriteKit's +y points UP. Every conversion goes through `scenePosition(_:)` and
`sceneRotation(_:)` in `GameScene`, and nowhere else. Bypassing them mirrors the whole game.
It looks plausible and plays backwards. The steered wheels and the wedge take an angle that
has already been converted.

**A drawn shape must be the shape it collides as.** Every truck node is positioned at the
centre of its collision box and built from the same `TruckDimensions`. The web build did not
do this, and every parked truck carried an invisible 0.83m kill zone down each side for
months. `RigTests` measures the box sizes rather than trusting them.

## What changed from the web build, on purpose

- **The throttle is analog.** Full deflection is the web build's key. Less is less. Easing
  off below the current speed brakes at the same 3.7g as letting go. `Rig.step` takes the
  throttle as -1 ... 1; the tests hold the full-deflection speeds to the web build's numbers.
- **Steering takes a target angle, not a nudge.** The thumb's position across the pad is the
  wheel angle. The rig's own steer rate still limits how fast the wheel gets there.
- **The speed dials are back**, clamped to the web build's ranges. The reverse ceiling of
  1.6x is held by a test: the fold must stay catchable at the top of the dial.
- **`centred(_:)` bounds the start pose by the rig's real extent.** The web version was
  wrong by 35px and clears the bottom wall partly by luck.

## Numbers worth not re-deriving

- Reverse is 24 km/h, capped by the fold clock: 2.3s of full lock before it jams.
- Forward is 40 km/h and costs nothing. The fold converges going forward.
- Braking is 3.7g, set by the lot and not by physics. There is 42cm between parked trucks and
  any scrape ends the run, so releasing the throttle has to stop inside about that.
- The fold stop at 83° is a tuned number. It is NOT derivable from the boxes.
- At the fold stop the rig is genuinely jammed and `step` refuses the whole timestep.
- The reverse point of no return is `asin((D/L) * tan(maxSteer))` = 64° / 40° / 27° for the
  three presets. Past it, reverse cannot recover. Only pulling forward can.
